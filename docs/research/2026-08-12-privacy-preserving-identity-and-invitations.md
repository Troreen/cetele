# Privacy-preserving identity, habit history, and invite-only signup

Research date: 2026-08-12

This note is product and engineering research, not legal advice. Before a real beta, the controller should confirm the lawful basis, Article 9 exception, privacy notice, retention schedule, processor arrangements, and any child-user requirements with Swedish/EU privacy counsel.

## Recommendation

For the current Supabase architecture, use a stable random Auth UUID as the internal owner of all records; collect no legal name; keep a user-chosen display alias separate from authentication; and, for the lowest-risk implementation, keep one verified email address only inside Supabase Auth for sign-in, verification, and recovery. Do not expose that email to mentors or use it as the profile identity. Supabase's documented permanent password identities are email or phone based, not username based, while anonymous identities cannot return to the same account after sign-out or loss of browser data. [Supabase users](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/users) [Supabase password auth](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/passwords) [Supabase identities](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/identities)

If the product requirement becomes **no email or phone at all**, treat that as a separate authentication-architecture decision. Current Supabase Auth documentation does not list username as a permanent identity provider, and Supabase explicitly discourages fake `username@domain` addresses. Its passkey support is experimental and currently requires an existing confirmed, non-anonymous user, so it does not yet remove the initial email/phone requirement. [Supabase identities](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/identities) [Supabase username/password warning](https://supabase.com/changelog/29370-supabase-auth-changes-to-default-email-provider) [Supabase passkeys](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/passkeys)

Use invite codes as an access gate, not as identity. A batch code may have `max_uses`, `uses`, `expires_at`, `revoked_at`, and an intended mentor/group, but each redemption should create an independent account and be consumed atomically. Store only a cryptographic hash of the code, rate-limit redemption attempts, never log the raw code or full redemption URL, and reject reuse after the limit or expiry. OWASP recommends that security tokens be cryptographically random, sufficiently long, securely stored, single-use where the workflow allows, and expiring; a multi-use beta code deliberately relaxes the single-use property and therefore needs a small use cap, short expiry, revocation, monitoring, and a safer single-use option for person-specific mentorship invitations. [OWASP Forgot Password Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Forgot_Password_Cheat_Sheet.html)

## Legal and retention implications

There is no general GDPR rule requiring an app to collect a legal name or email. The controller must instead define each processing purpose and lawful basis before collection, collect only what is necessary for that purpose, disclose the choice, and be able to demonstrate it. A private organisation may commonly consider contract, consent, legal obligation, or legitimate interests, but each purpose needs its own documented analysis. [GDPR Articles 5–6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj?locale=EN) [IMY lawful grounds](https://www.imy.se/en/organisations/data-protection/this-applies-accordning-to-gdpr/lawful-grounds-for-personal-data-processing/)

Çetele's spiritual habit names, completion patterns, notes, and mentorship graph may reveal religious or philosophical beliefs. Those are special-category personal data whose processing is prohibited unless an Article 9(2) exception applies. Explicit consent for specified purposes is one possible exception; a qualifying not-for-profit religious body may potentially rely on Article 9(2)(d), but only within its legitimate activities, with appropriate safeguards, for members/former members/regular contacts, and without disclosure outside the body absent consent. Ordinary Article 6 contract or legitimate-interest reasoning does not by itself satisfy Article 9. [GDPR Article 9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj?locale=EN) [IMY sensitive personal data](https://www.imy.se/en/organisations/data-protection/this-applies-accordning-to-gdpr/the-purposes-and-scope-of-gdpr/personuppgifter/sensitive-personal-data/)

Before production, document a risk analysis and decide whether a data protection impact assessment is required. IMY requires a DPIA whenever processing is likely to result in high risk; the combination of longitudinal sensitive data and hierarchical visibility makes a formal assessment prudent even before the product reaches the GDPR's explicit large-scale threshold. [IMY impact assessments](https://www.imy.se/en/organisations/data-protection/this-applies-accordning-to-gdpr/impact-assessments-and-prior-consultation/) [GDPR Article 35](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj?locale=EN)

Full habit history can be retained while an account is active because displaying long-term history is an explicit core purpose, not a vague future possibility. The privacy notice should say that clearly. Set and document a separate inactive-account review period; after account deletion or when the purpose ends, erase or truly anonymise the records unless a specific legal exception requires retention. Pseudonymous records that remain linkable to the person are not anonymous. [GDPR Articles 5 and 17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj?locale=EN) [IMY retention FAQ](https://www.imy.se/en/frequently-asked-questions/how-long-can-we-keep-personal-data/) [IMY right to erasure](https://www.imy.se/en/individuals/data-protection/your-rights-as-a-data-subject/?epslanguage=en)

A practical product policy to validate with counsel is: retain complete history while the account is active; notify and review after a clearly disclosed inactivity period; provide user-controlled export; offer deletion with a short, disclosed recovery grace period; then cascade-delete account-owned records and anonymise only those aggregate facts that remain genuinely non-identifying. GDPR portability can apply to data supplied by the user when automated processing is based on consent or contract. [GDPR Article 20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj?locale=EN) [Supabase user management](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/managing-user-data)

### Would a short rolling retention period make the processing GDPR-compliant?

No. Automatic deletion after, for example, 30, 90, or 180 days is a useful storage-minimisation safeguard, but it does not by itself make collection or use lawful. GDPR defines "processing" broadly to include collection, recording, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure, **and** erasure or destruction. Consequently, transient server-side or in-memory handling is still processing when personal data is received or used, even if it is never written to a database. The controller still needs a valid Article 6 basis, transparency, necessity, security, and all other applicable safeguards during that short lifecycle. Deleting later cannot retroactively cure collection or use that lacked those requirements. [GDPR Article 4(2) and Articles 5–6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/art_4/par_1/oj) [IMY personal data and processing](https://www.imy.se/en/organisations/data-protection/this-applies-accordning-to-gdpr/the-purposes-and-scope-of-gdpr/personal-data/) [IMY lawful grounds](https://www.imy.se/en/organisations/data-protection/this-applies-accordning-to-gdpr/lawful-grounds-for-personal-data-processing/)

The same is true for special-category data: if a habit record reveals religious or philosophical beliefs, an Article 9(2) exception is required while the record is collected, evaluated, shown to a mentor, or retained, even if it is deleted shortly afterward. A short lifetime reduces exposure and may help demonstrate proportionality; it does not replace Article 9. [GDPR Article 9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj?locale=EN) [IMY sensitive personal data](https://www.imy.se/en/organisations/data-protection/this-applies-accordning-to-gdpr/the-purposes-and-scope-of-gdpr/personuppgifter/sensitive-personal-data/)

Storage limitation is purpose-dependent, not a universal maximum. IMY says personal data may be retained only as long as needed for the stated purpose and should be deleted or de-identified when that purpose is fulfilled. A deliberately short rolling window is therefore appropriate only if Çetele's declared product purpose is recent accountability rather than durable personal history. If future history is itself a clearly described, necessary service feature, a longer period may be justifiable; the controller must still define deletion or periodic-review criteria and avoid keeping data for undefined future possibilities. [IMY retention FAQ](https://www.imy.se/en/frequently-asked-questions/how-long-can-we-keep-personal-data/) [IMY fundamental principles](https://www.imy.se/en/organisations/data-protection/this-applies-accordning-to-gdpr/the-gdpr-fundamental-principles/?epslanguage=en) [GDPR Article 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj?locale=EN)

True anonymisation can move the resulting data outside GDPR only when no person can be identified directly or indirectly. Removing the account ID, hashing it, substituting a UUID, or separating the lookup key is normally pseudonymisation if reconnection remains reasonably possible; the resulting records remain personal data. This is particularly important for sparse habit histories and mentorship graphs, whose distinctive patterns may permit re-identification even without a name. IMY notes that reliable anonymisation can be difficult, and the EDPB confirms that pseudonymised data linkable through additional information remains personal data. [IMY anonymous data](https://www.imy.se/en/organisations/data-protection/data-protection-within-different-areas/processing-of-personal-data--for-researchers/?epslanguage=en) [EDPB pseudonymisation guidance summary](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-adopts-pseudonymisation-guidelines-and-paves-the-way-to-improve-cooperation-with_en)

For a rolling-window version of Çetele, the practical contract should therefore say exactly what disappears and when: delete raw completions and notes older than the chosen window from live systems; ensure mentor views, exports, caches, analytics, logs, replicas, and backups follow a documented lifecycle; do not silently retain per-user streaks or aggregates that reconstruct the deleted history; and warn users that history beyond the window cannot be recovered. This design materially reduces risk, but the Article 6 and Article 9 analysis remains a launch prerequisite.

A small closed beta is still subject to GDPR; size mainly changes the scale-dependent obligations and risk, not the core duties. **Minimum gate before a small closed beta:** named controller and privacy contact; signed purpose/basis/Article 9 analysis by qualified counsel; documented ROPA; completed DPIA decision (and DPIA if the threshold is met—prudent here regardless); adult-only or resolved child pathway; privacy notice and consent/withdrawal mechanism if consent is selected; processor/transfer inventory and agreements; tested access controls, export, deletion/retention, backup restoration, and incident response; and no real data until those controls are verified. Invitation-only access reduces exposure but creates no GDPR exemption.

**Reassess before later scale or material change:** update the DPIA and purpose map when adding mentor levels, richer notes, automated inference, analytics/AI, new countries/vendors, minors, or materially more users. A DPO becomes mandatory if the controller's core activities involve large-scale regular/systematic monitoring or large-scale Article 9 processing; assess and document that threshold as the service grows. ## Invite and sign-up design

Recommended flow:

1. A mentor/admin creates either a single-use personal invitation or a small batch code with a use cap and expiry. The server returns the raw secret once; only its hash is persisted.
2. The invitee presents the secret, chooses a private verified email, password, and display alias. No name field appears.
3. A server transaction locks and validates the invitation, creates/links the Auth UUID, consumes one use, and establishes the intended mentorship relation. Concurrent redemptions cannot exceed the cap.
4. The email stays in Auth only. Application tables refer to the UUID; mentors see the alias, not email.
5. Generic responses, throttling, and CAPTCHA/risk controls protect invite redemption, signup, login, and recovery from enumeration and automated attacks. OWASP recommends generic authentication responses and throttling; Supabase exposes configurable Auth rate limits and CAPTCHA protection. [OWASP Authentication Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html) [Supabase production checklist](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/deployment/going-into-prod)

Supabase's native `inviteUserByEmail` and `generateLink({ type: 'invite' })` both require an email. `inviteUserByEmail` sends an email; `generateLink` lets a trusted server obtain an email action link for delivery through a custom channel, but it still creates an email-based identity. Both are server-only admin operations, and the secret key must never reach browser code. [Supabase invite users](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/users) [Supabase `generateLink`](https://supabase.com/docs/reference/javascript/auth-admin-generatelink)

For Çetele's proposed code-first experience, the cleanest fit is therefore an application-owned hashed invite table in front of ordinary Supabase email/password signup, not Supabase's email-invite API. Disable unrestricted signup or enforce invitation validation server-side so a code cannot be bypassed; Supabase exposes a global "Allow new users to sign up" control and a Before User Created hook that can reject creation under custom signup policy. [Supabase general Auth configuration](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/general-configuration) [Supabase Before User Created hook](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-hooks/before-user-created-hook)

## Decisions to make before implementation

1. Is a private recovery email acceptable if it is never visible to mentors and never copied into profiles? **Recommendation: yes for the first production version**, because it preserves durable multi-device access and uses the current supported Supabase path.
2. Are invite codes personal/single-use, batch/multi-use, or both? **Recommendation: both**, with single-use as the default for a specific mentor–student relationship and tightly capped batch codes only for controlled onboarding.
3. Who is the legal data controller, and which Article 9(2) exception covers the spiritual habit data? This must be resolved before accepting real user data.
4. Will minors use Çetele? If yes, perform a separate child-data and consent analysis before launch; children receive specific GDPR protections and information directed to them must be clear and age-appropriate. [IMY fundamental principles](https://www.imy.se/en/organisations/data-protection/this-applies-accordning-to-gdpr/the-gdpr-fundamental-principles/?epslanguage=en)
