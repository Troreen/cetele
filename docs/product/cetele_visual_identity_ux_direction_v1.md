# Çetele — Visual Identity & UX Direction (V1)

## Purpose of this document

This document defines the **high-level visual identity and UX direction** for Çetele.

It is intended to be combined with the separate product/feature brief and handed to a design/development agent.

This document **does not prescribe exact colors, fonts, component styles, spacing values, logo geometry, or implementation details**. Those should be explored and decided during design/development.

---

## 1. Overall direction

Çetele should feel:

- calm
- personal
- reflective
- grounded
- mature
- trustworthy
- modest
- quietly disciplined
- culturally Turkish
- openly spiritual, without becoming visually excessive

The preferred broad visual direction is closest to a **Quiet Journal** rather than a productivity dashboard.

However, this should be treated as an **emotional and brand direction**, not as a literal instruction to imitate paper notebooks, manuscripts, or historical interfaces.

Çetele should still feel like a contemporary web product.

A useful summary is:

> **Reflective in atmosphere, disciplined in action.**

---

## 2. What Çetele should not feel like

Çetele should avoid feeling:

- corporate
- clinical
- bureaucratic
- surveillance-oriented
- childish
- highly gamified
- competitive
- flashy
- hyper-productive
- like an enterprise analytics dashboard
- like a generic Western self-improvement app
- like a historical/Ottoman-themed novelty interface

The product should communicate seriousness without harshness.

---

## 3. Personal experience comes first

The student experience is first and foremost a **personal habit record**.

A student should primarily feel:

> **“This is my Çetele.”**

They should not feel that they are constantly filling out a report for someone else.

Although mentors can review a student's progress, the normal student experience should not continuously foreground this fact.

### Principle

> **Çetele is first a personal practice of self-accounting. Mentorship sits quietly behind it.**

The mentor should feel more like a safety net and source of guidance than an audience watching every action.

Students should clearly understand that their relevant progress is visible to their mentor, but the interface should not constantly remind them.

Avoid persistent elements such as:

- prominent mentor avatars on habit screens
- repeated “your mentor can see this” messaging
- review/read indicators beside ordinary habit completions
- countdowns to mentor reviews
- visual patterns associated with parental controls or monitoring systems

---

## 4. Core product philosophy in the UX

### Consistency over perfection

Çetele is built around the idea that **small actions performed consistently matter more than occasional bursts of effort**.

The interface should support this philosophy without turning it into a points system or streak obsession.

The historical record should show the truth clearly, including missed days, without visually shaming the user.

A missed day should communicate:

> “This is what happened.”

not:

> “You failed.”

---

## 5. The historical record is central

The long-term habit record is one of the most important parts of Çetele's identity.

Each habit has its own contribution-style completion grid.

The grid should feel like a **record of lived consistency**, not merely a statistic.

The product should naturally direct attention toward patterns over time.

A useful conceptual hierarchy is:

> **Habit → today → history**

rather than:

> **score → streak → achievement → history**

The record should ultimately help a person understand what they actually did over weeks and months.

---

## 6. Completion should feel satisfying, not gamified

Completing a habit should have a satisfying sense of finality and progress.

The interaction may eventually use subtle motion, visual feedback, or haptics, but the emotional reward should come from **adding another completed day to the record**, not from external rewards.

Avoid:

- XP
- coins
- levels
- leaderboards
- trophies
- excessive badges
- aggressive streak mechanics
- large celebratory confetti effects

The desired feeling is **quiet satisfaction**.

---

## 7. Student vs mentor experience

Student and mentor interfaces should belong to the same product and visual system, but their emphasis differs.

### Student side

The student side should feel:

- personal
- calm
- simple
- reflective
- easy to return to every day
- customizable enough for habits to feel like their own

Students may personalize habits through elements such as icon, color, and order.

The student experience should prioritize the person's relationship with their own habits and historical record.

### Mentor side

The mentor side naturally requires greater information density.

It should still feel calm and human rather than analytical or corporate.

The mentor experience should prioritize:

- people over metrics
- clarity over decoration
- identifying who may need attention
- understanding consistency over time
- supporting daily mentor responsibility

A useful direction is:

> **The student side feels personal. The mentor side feels composed and relational.**

Mentor views should not resemble an enterprise performance-management dashboard.

---

## 8. Turkish identity

Çetele V1 is being developed specifically for Turkish users and should feel **genuinely Turkish**.

This does not mean nationalist branding.

Turkishness can influence the product's overall cultural character, language, tone, restraint, references, and design inspiration.

Traditional Turkish influence is welcome when appropriate, but it should be translated into a contemporary product rather than copied literally.

### Explicitly avoid

- Turkish flags used decoratively
- patriotic/state imagery
- nationalist symbolism
- a forced red-and-white national color scheme
- government/institutional visual language

### Direction

> **Culturally Turkish, not nationalist. Traditional influence, not historical imitation.**

---

## 9. Spiritual and religious identity

Çetele is openly designed for **religious and spiritual mentorship**.

Its spiritual purpose should therefore not be hidden behind a completely neutral productivity-app identity.

The broader inspiration behind the project includes the works and teachings of **Fethullah Gülen**, alongside relevant Islamic spiritual and intellectual sources such as **Said Nursi** and hadith.

However, spiritual identity should be expressed with restraint and dignity.

The product should not rely on religious decoration simply to signal that it is religious.

Avoid unnecessary use of:

- decorative Arabic calligraphy
- mosque imagery
- repeated Islamic ornament
- religious motifs placed into ordinary UI without purpose

### Principle

> **Spiritual content should reinforce the meaning of Çetele, not decorate the interface.**

---

## 10. Spiritual content in V1

For V1, spiritual content should remain very simple.

The current direction is:

> **One carefully selected quote at the top of the daily view.**

The quote may come from an appropriate:

- hadith
- passage or quotation from Fethullah Gülen
- passage or quotation from Said Nursi

The quote should act as a quiet spiritual frame for the daily experience.

It should not overpower the habits themselves or make the screen feel like a motivational-content feed.

Exact quotations, translations, wording, and attribution should be verified before use.

There is no need in V1 for personalized quotes, habit-specific religious messages, or a large quote system.

---

## 11. Brand strength

Çetele should have a **distinctive but restrained** identity.

The product should eventually be recognizable as Çetele, but branding should not dominate ordinary usage.

The student's habits and record are more important than repeatedly showing the brand.

The name **Çetele** itself should be allowed to carry significant identity, especially because V1 is fully Turkish.

The exact:

- logo
- symbol
- wordmark
- visual motif

should be explored with the design/development agent rather than prescribed here.

A suitable brief for that exploration is:

> **Develop a simple, recognizable Çetele identity that fits the Turkish, spiritual, personal, reflective, and disciplined character of the product without becoming decorative or over-branded.**

---

## 12. Emotional relationship with the user

The product should encourage internal accountability rather than dependence on external monitoring.

Over time, the ideal motivation is:

> **“I do this because this is the person I am trying to become.”**

rather than:

> **“I need to do this because my mentor will see it.”**

Mentorship can support the first motivation, but should not replace it.

The student should feel that Çetele belongs to them.

---

## 13. High-level identity themes

Three ideas summarize the current direction well:

### Muhasebe — personal self-accounting

Çetele helps a person look truthfully at their own consistency.

### İstikrar — steady, sustained effort

Small continued actions matter more than perfection.

### Rehberlik — human guidance

A trusted mentor is present when support or attention is needed, without dominating the student's daily experience.

These ideas should guide product decisions but do not need to appear as literal labels throughout the interface.

---

## 14. Accessibility and emotional safety

Even before exact visual decisions are made, several principles should be maintained:

- state meaning should never depend on color alone
- completed, missed, excused, attention-needed, reviewed, and related states should remain understandable at a glance
- text should remain comfortably readable
- interaction targets should work well on mobile
- the interface should avoid visually punishing users for missed habits
- mentor-related states should avoid creating an atmosphere of surveillance
- visual personalization should not reduce clarity or accessibility

Exact accessibility specifications can be handled during design/development.

---

## 15. Mobile-first character

Çetele is a daily-use product, so the student experience in particular should feel natural on mobile.

High-level priorities:

- completing today's habits should be fast
- the current state should be immediately understandable
- contribution grids should remain useful on smaller screens
- personalization should not create clutter
- the daily experience should not feel like navigating a desktop dashboard squeezed onto a phone

Exact responsive behavior is left to the design/development phase.

---

## 16. Design decisions deliberately left open

The following should **not** be treated as finalized by this document:

- exact color palette
- brand colors
- typography and font selection
- serif vs sans-serif decisions
- border radius
- card treatment
- precise spacing system
- icon library
- exact contribution-grid styling
- precise state colors
- shadows
- textures
- animation specifications
- light/dark mode execution
- final logo or wordmark
- exact use of traditional Turkish visual references
- component architecture
- screen layouts

The design/development agent should explore these details while respecting the principles in this document.

---

## 17. Useful visual references

### HabitKit

Primary inspiration for:

- simplicity
- habit cards
- contribution grids
- satisfying daily completion
- allowing habits to have visual personality

Çetele should learn from HabitKit without simply copying its visual identity.

### GitHub contribution grids

Useful reference for representing consistent activity across time.

Çetele's grids should ultimately feel more personal and habit-oriented than GitHub's developer-focused implementation.

### Quiet journals / personal notebooks

Useful as an **emotional reference** for calmness, reflection, permanence, and personal ownership.

This should not be interpreted literally as requiring paper textures, handwriting, or skeuomorphic notebook UI.

---

## 18. Summary for the design/development agent

Çetele is a Turkish daily habit accountability system built for personal spiritual development and mentorship.

The student experience should feel first and foremost like **their own personal record**. Mentorship is important, but it should remain quietly in the background of everyday use.

The product should feel calm, reflective, mature, spiritually grounded, and culturally Turkish. It should lean toward the emotional character of a quiet personal journal while remaining a contemporary web product.

Consistency matters more than perfection. Historical habit records are central. Completion should feel satisfying without gamification.

Religious and spiritual identity may be explicit, but should be expressed with restraint. In V1, a single carefully selected spiritual quotation at the top of the daily view is sufficient.

The brand should be recognizable but restrained. Avoid nationalist imagery, excessive religious decoration, surveillance aesthetics, enterprise-dashboard styling, and game-like reward systems.

Exact visual design choices should be explored during development rather than treated as fixed by this brief.
